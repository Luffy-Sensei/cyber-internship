SCENARIO 1: VISHING
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Channel: Voice Call
Target: Sqrock IT
Impersonated Role: HR Representative
Pretext: System Update Required

PSYCHOLOGICAL TRIGGERS:
  • Authority: The scenario relies on an apparently trusted role or organization to increase perceived legitimacy.
  • Fear: The scenario introduces a possible negative consequence to encourage immediate action.
  • Liking: The scenario uses friendliness, rapport, or common interests to increase trust.

SCRIPT:
  [PHASE 1 OPENER]
  Caller claims to be HR Representative from Sqrock IT. Uses professional tone to establish credibility.
  [PHASE 2 PRETEXT]
  The caller explains: 'System Update Required' and states this requires immediate attention.
  [PHASE 3 HOOK]
  The scenario introduces an account or security concern designed to create urgency and fear.
  [PHASE 4 REQUEST]
  The simulated caller attempts to obtain sensitive information such as passwords, OTPs, or account details.
  [PHASE 5 PRESSURE]
  If met with resistance, the caller increases urgency by threatening account suspension or security breaches.
  [AWARENESS NOTE]
  This script demonstrates how vishing attacks use authority and fear to manipulate victims into sharing sensitive data.

RED FLAGS:
  • Credential Request (critical): A caller requesting passwords, authentication codes, or other secrets is a major warning sign.
  • Unexpected Contact (high): Unsolicited calls about account problems should be independently verified.
  • Authority Impersonation (high): Claiming to represent IT, security, a bank, or another trusted authority can be used to create false trust.
  • Urgency or Pressure (medium): Pressure to act immediately can prevent the recipient from independently verifying the request.
  • Threats of Consequences (critical): Threatening account suspension, legal action, or other consequences is a common manipulation tactic.

DEFENSIVE GUIDANCE:
  • Never disclose secrets: Never share passwords, authentication codes, or security questions over the phone.
  • Verify independently: End the call and contact the organization using official phone numbers from their website.
  • Don't trust caller ID: Caller ID can be spoofed to display any number the attacker chooses.
  • Take your time: Legitimate organizations will not pressure you to make immediate decisions.
  • Report suspicious calls: Report suspicious social-engineering attempts to your security team or IT department.

SCENARIO 2: SMISHING
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Channel: SMS/Text Message
Target: Sqrock IT
Impersonated Role: Mobile Carrier
Pretext: Suspicious Login Detected

PSYCHOLOGICAL TRIGGERS:
  • Authority: The scenario relies on an apparently trusted role or organization to increase perceived legitimacy.
  • Urgency: The scenario pressures the target to act quickly without thinking critically.
  • Scarcity: The scenario presents a limited-time opportunity or consequence to create artificial value.

SCRIPT:
  [MESSAGE CONTENT]
  [Sqrock IT] ALERT: Suspicious Login Detected. Verify your account immediately: https://secure-login-verify.net/sqrock-it-suspicious-login-detected-a5eb10
  [PRETEXT]
  The message claims that action is required because of: Suspicious Login Detected.
  [HOOK]
  The message uses urgency and fear to encourage the recipient to click the link without verification.
  [REQUEST]
  The link leads to a fake login page designed to steal credentials or install malware.
  [AWARENESS NOTE]
  This demonstrates how smishing uses shortened URLs and urgency to trick victims into clicking malicious links.

RED FLAGS:
  • Suspicious Link (critical): Unexpected links in SMS messages should not be trusted without independent verification.
  • Unexpected Message (high): An unsolicited security or account notification should be verified through an official channel.
  • Urgency or Pressure (high): Threats of immediate account suspension can encourage impulsive clicking.
  • Sender Impersonation (high): A message may claim to represent a trusted organization without actually originating from it.
  • Too Good to Be True (medium): Promises of prizes, refunds, or rewards are often used to entice victims to click malicious links.

DEFENSIVE GUIDANCE:
  • Don't click links: Never click links in unexpected SMS messages, even if they appear legitimate.
  • Verify through official apps: Check account status through the organization's official website or mobile application.
  • Don't reply to suspicious texts: Replying confirms your number is active and may lead to more attacks.
  • Block and report: Block the sender and report the message to your mobile carrier and security team.
  • Forward to spam services: Forward suspicious texts to 7726 (SPAM) in the US or your country's equivalent.
